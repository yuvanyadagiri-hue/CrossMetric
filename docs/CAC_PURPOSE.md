# Purpose

**CrossMetric uses public transportation and school data to identify which roads and intersections around a Georgia school have the strongest evidence for professional safety review.**

## The problem

Georgia already collects detailed information about crashes, injuries, pedestrian involvement, traffic volume and road characteristics, but it is spread across separate systems built for engineers. A principal, a parent group or a county commissioner cannot easily answer a simple question: *around this school, where should a professional safety review start?* Safety review budgets are limited, so that question matters.

## Who it serves

- **School administrators** preparing safe-routes conversations with their district or county
- **Transportation planners** screening many schools to decide where field reviews are most valuable
- **Community safety organizations** making evidence-based requests to local government
- **Residents and students** who want to understand the roads they use every day

## What makes it responsible

- It **prioritizes**; it does not predict crashes or declare roads unsafe. Final decisions belong to professionals.
- Every score is **explained** and every number is **traceable** to its public source, down to individual GDOT crash report IDs.
- It is honest about uncertainty: missing data is shown as unavailable, not zero; rankings are tested under alternative assumptions; validation results are published, including where a simpler method does better.
- It collects **no personal data**: no accounts, no tracking, no student information.

## Impact

CrossMetric turns fragmented public data into evidence communities can use to decide where professional safety review may be most valuable, for every one of Georgia's 2,336 public schools.
